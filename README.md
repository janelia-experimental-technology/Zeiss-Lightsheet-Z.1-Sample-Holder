<h1>Z.1-Compatible Sample Holder</h1>

</div>

## Abstract

Selective Plane Illumination Microscopy (SPIM) is a powerful tool for volumetric imaging of cleared specimens. The Zeiss Lightsheet microscope Z.1 offers a commercial solution for SPIM imaging. The Z.1 presents sample mounting challenges, though. Users must position samples vertically and stabilize them for hours-long imaging sessions. We offer designs for a custom sample holder to mount cleared and expansion microscopy (ExM) samples on the Z.1 system.

## Purpose

A disposable or semi-disposable sample holder (shown in red in Figure 2) was needed to interface with the rotor post of the Z.1 microscope (shown in yellow in Figure 2). Two final versions have been developed - one to be printed and the other to be machined. The machined version will have a higher setup cost, but will be cheaper and more reliable in the long run.

Issues faced during development included wildly inconsistent tolerances between batches of printed lightsheet holders and deformations of the printed part during and after printing. Some major causes of tolerance issues include: the calibration state of the printer; support material residue on the printed part after post-processing; the orientation of the part in the printer; and the inconsistent pressure applied by the locating pin of the rotor post on the sample holder. Major causes of mid- and post-printing deformations were the orientation of the part in the printer and exposure of the printed parts to raw resin vapors and IPA cleaning solution.

Due to the frustration in achieving quality results with printed sample holders, a machined sample holder was designed to avoid printing process woes. The machined holder consists of a reusable metal base and a semi-disposable plastic platform. A new printed version was also worked up with new features that help mitigate some issues faced with the old printed versions.

## Printed Version (V18R)

**Parts:**

- LIGHTSHEETHOLDER V18R

**Files:**

- V18R.STP
- V18R.STL
- V18R.PDF
- V18R - PRINTING DIRECTION.PDF

The advantage of the 3D printed version is that it can be fabricated in-house with a basic resin printer in one part without any assembly needed. Formlabs printers with Formlabs Clear resin are known to produce good results for these holders. Cheaper resin printers, like the Elegoo Mars, Anycubic Photon, or Phrozen Sonic Mini with AnyCubic Clear, NOVA3D Ultra-Clear, or Siraya Tech Blu Clear V2 resin, may also work well.

The printed version is known to work well when printed in Veroclear or Formlabs Clear, although Veroclear is recommended as the material is less susceptible to shattering on impact. Orientation is critical if the part is printed in Veroclear on a Polyjet printer as the material can swell anisotropically with repeated, prolonged exposure to saline solutions. Orientation does not matter when printed in Formlabs Clear and swelling is not observed with the Formlabs material, though there can be some unwanted deformation right off the printer due to the lower rigidity of the printer and absorption of resin vapors and IPA cleaning solution while the part is in its green state.

Forward the STL <u>or</u> STP file to your 3D printing service to have it 3D printed. The PDF file is also provided to help clarify the scale of the part for the printing service. If VeroClear is chosen, then see the "V18R - PRINTING DIRECTION.PDF" file for for the correct orientation or forward that document to your 3D printing service.

Any printing service capable of printing VeroClear will be able to process your parts, but Xometry is known to produce quality parts with quick turnaround.

## Machined Version (V20M)

**Parts:**

- LIGHTSHEETHOLDER V20M - BASE
- LIGHTSHEETHOLDER V20M - PLATFORM
- 2X 91771A054 0-80x3/16" Stainless Steel Pan Head Screw (from McMaster.com) https://www.mcmaster.com/91772A054/

**Files:**

- V20M - ASSEMBLY.PDF
- V20M - BASE.PDF
- V20M - BASE.STL
- V20M - BASE.STP
- V20M - PLATFORM.DXF
- V20M - PLATFORM.PDF
- V20M - PLATFORM.STP

The advantage of the machined version is that the part of the 3D-printed version of the holder that is most expensive to post-process has been confined to a durable, reusable, machinable part. The platform, onto which the samples are mounted, is a disposable acrylic part that can be lasercut in-house or cheaply lasercut by an outside service. The platform is held onto the base by a couple of 0-80 x 3/16" pan head screws.

Forward the STP <u>and</u> PDF files for the Base to your machining service to have the Base component fabricated. Forward the STP or DXF file of the Platform to your lasercutting service to have the Platform component fabricated.

Any prototyping fabricator should be able to take care of the Base part for you, but either Xometry or Protolabs are recommended. The Platform part is most cost effective if it can be lasercut in-house en masse from acrylic, but it can also be sent out for lasercutting, waterjetting, or machining from acrylic, polycarbonate, or stainless steel if an in-house lasercutter is not available. SendCutSend or Xometry is recommended for the platform.
